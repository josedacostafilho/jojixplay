# Phone play

An adult rotates the phone to landscape, sets up optional external screen mirroring and taps **Ligar a câmera** to grant camera access. The camera's picture then fills the screen with a button for each of the phone's cameras (**Frente 1**, **Trás 1** and so on) and **Começar**: the adult tries them, keeps the one that shows the whole body in that room, which in a small room is usually a wide camera on the back, and taps **Começar**. The phone remembers that camera for next time. With a back camera the phone stands with its back to the room. The phone stays beside the television; every subsequent menu, help control, game selection and confirmation supports movement.

Outside games the mirrored camera fills the whole viewport, cropped to fit and never stretched, and the menu hangs on the player's body. Three games float in a row above the head. Raise a hand to one and hold for 800 ms to play. A bubble sits at arm's length on each side at shoulder height: stretch an arm to one to move the row along by one game, and keep holding to keep moving. A small picture at each end of the row shows the game that comes next and cannot be chosen; a place with no game yet is drawn dashed. Buttons grow as the player comes closer, up to a share of the screen height. When the shoulders are high on screen the bubbles slide down to stay clear of the row. A hold carries on through a moment of lost tracking, and a hand raised straight onto a button holds it. With arms hanging, nothing can be selected.

The objects are placed in shoulder widths from the shoulders of the most central person, so they follow the player and suit both a child and an adult. Both shoulders must be visible; otherwise a single prompt asks the player to face the camera and nothing is selectable. A ring marks each tracked hand at the estimated index position (coarse index observation, otherwise a short forearm extension, otherwise slightly above the wrist, bounded to 8% of frame height). That same point is tested against the objects. Menus have no stick figure or body avatar.

Choosing Desenhar turns the bubbles into **1 pessoa** and **2 pessoas**, with the card as the way back; the host applies one-/two-person inference before mounting the game. Corrida opens after one-person inference is applied. Confirmed game exit returns to the menu. **Sair**, a small target in the bottom-left corner, ends the session after a two-second hold. Turning, selecting and going back each make a short synthesized sound. Games decide their own visuals and reuse the same capture; the camera image is hidden inside a game unless the game asks the host to show it. A game may also ask the host to sense hands instead of bodies while it runs; the menu senses bodies again before another game can be opened. The shelf also holds [Sensores](sensores.md), the owner's sensing bench.

Two screens are used with the phone in hand. The start screen has three setup steps (mirror to the TV, rest the phone in landscape, make room), a privacy line and **Ligar a câmera**. The camera screen that follows has only the picture, the cameras, **Cancelar** and **Começar**; a camera that will not open says so and the previous one stays ([ADR-0037](../decisions/0037-camera-chosen-by-looking.md)). It carries no game instructions; each game explains itself. The menu keeps a one-line capture-age readout for tuning. Camera pixels and coordinates are never recorded, stored, logged or transmitted; the chosen camera's name is the one thing kept on the phone. No microphone, account, backend or in-app television transport exists.

Explicit stop, portrait, leaving for another app or tab, camera failure and unmount release capture, inference, immersive state and render resources. A new capture requires trusted setup again. Model accuracy, alignment, heat and external-mirroring latency still require target-phone acceptance. Browser tests verify lifecycle, crop math and synthetic movement navigation, not physical tracking quality.
