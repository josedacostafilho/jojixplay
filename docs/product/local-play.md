# Phone play

An adult rotates the phone to landscape, sets up optional external screen mirroring and taps **Ligar a câmera** to grant camera access. The phone stays beside the television; every subsequent menu, help control, game selection and confirmation supports movement.

Outside games the mirrored camera fills the whole viewport, cropped to fit and never stretched, and the menu hangs on the player's body. The game in focus floats above the head. Raise a hand to it and hold for 800 ms to play. A bubble sits at arm's length on each side at shoulder height: stretch an arm to one to see the previous or next game, and keep holding to keep turning. Other games show beside the card as pictures only. With arms hanging, nothing can be selected.

The objects are placed in shoulder widths from the shoulders of the most central person, so they follow the player and suit both a child and an adult. Both shoulders must be visible; otherwise a single prompt asks the player to face the camera and nothing is selectable. A ring marks each tracked hand at the estimated index position (coarse index observation, otherwise a short forearm extension, otherwise slightly above the wrist, bounded to 8% of frame height). That same point is tested against the objects. Menus have no stick figure or body avatar.

Choosing Desenhar turns the bubbles into **1 pessoa** and **2 pessoas**, with the card as the way back; the host applies one-/two-person inference before mounting the game. Corrida opens after one-person inference is applied. Confirmed game exit returns to the menu. **Sair**, a small target in the bottom-left corner, ends the session after a two-second hold. Turning, selecting and going back each make a short synthesized sound. Games decide their own visuals and hide the camera presentation while reusing the same capture.

The start screen is the only one used with the phone in hand: three setup steps (mirror to the TV, rest the phone in landscape, make room), a privacy line and **Ligar a câmera**. It carries no game instructions; each game explains itself. The menu keeps a one-line capture-age readout for tuning. Camera pixels and coordinates are never recorded, stored, logged or transmitted. No microphone, account, backend or in-app television transport exists.

Explicit stop, portrait, camera failure and unmount release capture, inference, immersive state and render resources. A new capture requires trusted setup again. Model accuracy, alignment, heat and external-mirroring latency still require target-phone acceptance. Browser tests verify lifecycle, crop math and synthetic movement navigation, not physical tracking quality.
